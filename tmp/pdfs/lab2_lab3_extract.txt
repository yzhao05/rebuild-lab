Lab 2 Magnetostatics 
1.1 What is this lab about? 
Today, we will focus on playing with static magnetic fields. In today's experiment, we 
will utilize advanced virtual reality (VR) technology to systematically observe and analyze 
the distribution characteristics of magnetic fields generated by DC (or quasi-static) line 
currents and helical coils (solenoids). Additionally, we will delve into the coupling of multiple 
current sources in affecting the structure of magnetic fields. Through a series of meticulous 
experimental designs, our aim is to enhance your understanding of the fundamental properties 
of static magnetic fields, laying a solid foundation for future scientific research and practical 
applications. 
Learning Objectives 
⚫ Understand Magnetic Field Fundamentals: Gain a comprehensive understanding of 
the basic principles of magnetic fields, including the concept of magnetic flux density, 
magnetic field lines, the boundary conditions for magnetic fields, and the behavior of 
magnetic fields around conductors. 
⚫ Visualize Magnetic Field Distributions: Utilize VR technology to visualize and 
interpret the magnetic field patterns produced by straight wires and helical coils when 
they are energized. This will enable you to appreciate the three-dimensional nature of 
magnetic fields and how they vary with currents. 
⚫ Analyze Magnetic Field Coupling: Observe and analyze the changes in magnetic fields 
when multiple current sources coexist. This will help you understand the superposition 
principle of magnetic fields and how they can reinforce or cancel each other. 
⚫ Apply Ampere's Law: Relate the observed magnetic field distributions to Ampere's Law 
to static magnetic fields, reinforcing your theoretical knowledge with practical 
observations. 
⚫ Develop Experimental Skills: Enhance your VR experimental skills for magnetic field 
visualization, collect and interpret data, and draw conclusions based on your 
observations. 
1.2 Theories and Fundamentals 
The static magnetic fields, i.e., the magnetic fields associated with DC currents, are 
governed by the Ampere’s law and the Gauss’s law, illustrated in differential form as follows, 
---page 2---
∇ × 𝐻̅ = 𝐽̅ 
∇ ⋅ 𝐵̅ = 0 
in which 𝐽̅ denotes the free current density, and 𝐵̅ and 𝐻̅ represent the magnetic flux density 
and the magnetic field, respectively, and they are linked through the constative relation  
𝐵̅ = 𝜇𝐻̅ 
in a linear magnetic material with permeability 𝜇.  
For time-varying fields, the right-hand-side to the Ampere’s law is to be expanded with a 
new term 𝜕𝐷̅/𝜕𝑡, representing displacement current in vacuum and also accounting for 
polarization current in a dielectric material. But in quasi-static scenarios, for which the waves 
travel in significantly larger distance during the time-variation scale compared to the structure 
of interest, we can drop this term 𝜕𝐷̅/𝜕𝑡 while allowing 𝐵̅ and 𝐻̅ to vary with time. So we 
can still apply the DC field picture to approximate the quasi-static magnetic field behavior. 
The magnetic fields are solenoidal with the associated field lines forming loops with no 
starting or ends. The relation between the current source 𝐽̅ and the associated magnetic fields 
is linear such that we can apply vector superposition principle for fields corresponding to 
composite current sources.  
In ECE 329, we derived the DC magnetic fields corresponding to an infinitely long and 
straight line-currents and an infinitely long solenoid. For an infinite solenoid, the magnetic 
fields are confined within the solenoid. In practice, we have solenoid of finite length where 
the magnetic field lines leak outside the solenoid to form loops. In this lab, we are making use 
of the infinite line currents and solenoids of finite lengths as basic current carrying elements 
to illustrate the corresponding field patterns. The current sources are assumed to be placed in 
free space with permeability 𝜇0.

1.5 Further Development on The Lab 
Our VR lab for magnetostatics is far from perfect in its current stage, and we rely on you 
to further polish the labs. Below we have listed some of the potential directions that we are 
aiming at in improving this lab. You can take these development tasks as optional course 
projects to earn you bonus credits if you have interest in VR technology and in applying it in 

---page 10---
electromagnetic education and research. A few aspects are summarized below for your 
reference. You can also come up ideas on your own and discuss with us.   
• Currently we only support two kinds of elementary current distributions. We want to 
extend these to more current source elements including but not limited to a current 
sheet, a single current loop/coil (i.e., a magnetic dipole), and a solenoid of varying 
lengths (from a short length to infinity), etc. We also want to be able to position the 
current precisely in the scene and have methods to tune the current magnitude 
continuously. 
• We want to enhance the freedom in choosing the observation viewpoint on the fields. 
Currently, the observers are limited to move on the floor, and the observers need to 
climb up physically to view the fields from top. We hope to extend the viewpoint to 
potentially anywhere in space, including above or below the ground, or dive inside 
the solenoid, etc., to improve the flexibility to observe the fields.  
We want to have better and more flexible visualization on the fields. We can 
have more options beyond showing the field lines. We want to visualize the direction 
of the fields beyond its magnitudes. We want to have options to visualize the 
magnetic vector potential 𝐴̅ as well. We want to develop methods allowing us to 
position the current sources anywhere in space not limited by the visualization cube 
while only observe the fields in the field visualization region.

Lab 3 Wave Polarization 
1.1 What is this lab about 
In today's experiment, we will embark on a fascinating exploration of wave polarization 
together. Using advanced VR technology, you will experience firsthand the process of 
polarization state transformation as a plane wave passes through linear polarizers and quarter-
wave plates. Through a carefully designed combination of polarization elements experiment, 
a variety of diverse polarization phenomena will be presented to you, not only deepening your 
understanding of the core concepts of wave polarization, but also offering you a direct and 
educational hands-on experience.  
This experiment aims to not only solidify your understanding of the fundamental 
principles and concepts of wave polarization through a series of vivid and intuitive 
demonstrations, but also to cultivate your observation skills, rigorous scientific thinking, and 
problem-solving abilities through hands-on operation and observation. Let's immerse 
ourselves in this journey of exciting exploration and unveil the mysteries of wave polarization 
together, experiencing the scientific beauty behind it.  
Learning Objectives 
Understand the basic concepts of polarization: Through the experiment, students will 
gain a deep understanding of the fundamental concepts and properties of wave polarization, 
including linear polarization, circular polarization, elliptical polarization, etc., and recognize 
that polarization is an important characteristic of electromagnetic waves. 
Master the operating principles of polarization components: Understand the 
structure, working principles of linear polarizers and quarter-wave plates, as well as their 
impact on the polarization state of waves, especially how quarter-wave plates convert linearly 
polarized light into elliptically or circularly polarized light. 
Observe and analyze polarization phenomena: Utilizing VR technology, students will 
visually observe changes in the polarization state of waves after passing through different 
combinations of polarization components, learn to analyze the physical principles behind 
these changes, and enhance their observation and analytical skills.

1.2 Theories and Fundamentals 
Wave polarizations are about the vector directions of the fields and their evolution with 
time. We typically associate the wave polarization to the direction of the E fields of a wave. 
Consider a wave traveling along 𝑧̂ direction in a lossless medium, a monochromatic wave 
can be represented in its phasor

assuming real relative phase angle 𝜙, ratio 𝜉, and phase constant 𝛽, while 𝐸0 can be 
complex. 𝑎̂ 1, 𝑎̂ 2, and 𝑧̂ are mutually perpendicular forming the basis of a right-handed 
Cartesian coordinate.

a right-hand elliptically polarized (RHEP) wave for 𝜉 ≠ 1 and 𝜉 ≠ 0.  
(iii) If 𝜙 = −

2, we have a left-hand circularly polarized (LHCP) wave for 𝜉 = 1 
and a left-hand elliptically polarized (LHEP) wave for 𝜉 ≠ 1 and 𝜉 ≠ 0. 
(iv) For other values of 𝜙, we in general have an elliptically polarized wave with 
semiaxis of the ellipse off the 𝑎̂ 1 and 𝑎̂ 2 axes.  
When the above wave transmits through a perfect polarizer with polarization 
transformation tensor 𝜎̅̅, the output wave polarization becomes (𝑎̂ 1𝑒𝑗𝜙 + 𝑎̂ 2𝜉) ⋅ 𝜎̅̅.

(ii) For an ideal quarter-wave plate as characterized by fast axis 𝑓̂ and slow axis 𝑠̂, 
with no reflective and absorption losses,

Note the wave component polarized along the fast axis 𝑓̂ exceeds the 
component polarized along the slow axis 𝑠̂ by 90∘ after passing through the 
quarter-wave plate. Quarter-wave plates are typically made of birefringent 
materials associated with a tensor permittivity, which allows waves polarized 
along different directions travel with different phase velocities (and phase 
constants) in the medium. 
(iii) When we cascade a polarizer with polarization transformation tensor 𝜎̅̅1 and the 
2nd polarizer with transformation tensor 𝜎̅̅2, the resulting overall transformation 
tensor is 𝜎̅̅ = 𝜎̅̅1 ⋅ 𝜎̅̅2, and in general 𝜎̅̅1 ⋅ 𝜎̅̅2 ≠ 𝜎̅̅2 ⋅ 𝜎̅̅1. Thus, we cannot

1.5 Further Development on The Lab 
Our VR lab for wave polarization is far from perfect in its current stage, and we rely on 
you to further polish it. Below we have listed some of the potential directions that we are 
aiming at in improving this lab for your reference. You can take these development tasks as 
optional course projects to earn you bonus credits if you have interest in VR technology and 
in applying it to electromagnetic education and research. You can also come up ideas on your 
own and discuss with us.   
• We want to have a better labeling on the polarizers about their featured axes. 
These include the direction of polarization for the linear polarizer and the fast 
and slow axis for the quarter-wave plate. We also plan to increase the electrical 
length (with respect to wavelengths) of the field observation stage such that the 
circular and elliptical polarization can be better visualized. Besides the electric 
field, we also hope to illustrate the polarization of the magnetic field. We also 
want to have a half-wave plate option (i.e., cascading two quarter-wave plates) 
in the wave polarizers, and we hope to have freedoms in choosing the incident 
wave polarizations.  
• It is desired to combine the wave polarization illustration with wave reflection 
and transmission. We hope to design experiments for the students to observe the 
polarization for the reflected and transmitted waves. The standing waves due to 
the superposition of incident wave and reflected wave can also be illustrated.
